# Nick Hakim’s I Can See Stays Softly Stuck

Source · 2026-07-30 · (no link)

## Lead
- Pitchfork’s review of Nick Hakim’s fourth studio album, *I Can See*, argues that the record lives in a mild middle ground of moderate tempos and subdued vocals.
- Brief flashes of tension and texture appear, the critic says, but they are often smoothed into a pleasant haze rather than pushed into real drama.

### 英文改写 Readable rewrite
Near the end of “Soft Wet Clay,” a brooding ballad on Nick Hakim’s new album, something nearly thrilling almost takes shape. Most instruments fall away, leaving a heavily filtered drum kit and a piano that plunks dissonant chords. The duet seems to sag and slow, then suddenly gathers speed and swagger—only to cut out just as the song feels ready to move somewhere new.

Hakim has long built beautiful songs and then gently glitched, stretched, and gummed them up. Since his 2014 debut EP *Where Will We Go*, he has shaped a style that mixes sepia-toned psych rock with Soulquarians-style modern R&B, letting live instruments and MPCs bounce against each other until the groove sits between the human and the machine. Those off-kilter production touches often carry the turmoil that his relaxed singing and unshowy playing keep quiet. On “QADIR,” a highlight from his 2020 album *WILL THIS MAKE ME GOOD*, a demonic pitched-down double of his voice and a choir of wordless cries push icy dread into hot desperation.

*I Can See*, his fourth studio album, largely lacks that kind of drama, and the review says it suffers for it. The songs inhabit a balmy medium zone between sonic gunk and clarity, between sharp hook and vague atmosphere. Tempos stay relentlessly moderate; Hakim’s voice is more subdued than ever, both in delivery and in the mix. Careful listening still turns up tension—a synth-bass swoop that unsettles the chorus of “The Reason,” a piano-and-synth riff on “Endlessly Waiting” that tick-tocks in close voicings, and vocals on the title track that may be lightly Chipmunked. In general, though, the scuzz is homogenized and spread evenly. A cassette-like hiss opens the album and wraps every song in pleasant degradation. Promotional copy calls the record “medicinal”; if so, the critic suggests, the medicine is probably a soporific.

With its love of repetition, its allergy to extremes, and its focus on the feel of sound—Hakim and longtime collaborator Andrew Sarlo want you to hear the creak of keys and the rumble of sawtooth waves—*I Can See* looks ready for neo-soul minimalism. Hakim has named Steve Reich and Terry Riley as influences, yet these songs rarely build; they mostly proceed, ending more or less where they begin. Still, after headphones in the woods, kitchen speakers, car stereo, and laptop listening with eyes closed, the reviewer found a minor miracle: by always retreating into the middle distance of attention, the album somehow made itself new with each listen.

### 中文全文翻译
在 Nick Hakim 新专辑后半段的阴郁叙事曲《Soft Wet Clay》将近结尾时，几乎要出现令人振奋的瞬间。多数乐器退场，只剩重度滤波的鼓组和敲出不协和和弦的钢琴。这组二重奏仿佛塌软、变慢，随即突然加快、带上气势——却偏偏在歌曲似乎要冲向新处时戛然而止。

Hakim 一向擅长写出优美歌曲，再轻轻做毛刺、拉伸、搅乱。自 2014 年出道 EP《Where Will We Go》以来，他形成了一种介于泛黄迷幻摇滚与 Soulquarians 式当代 R&B 之间的风格，让现场乐器与 MPC 彼此碰撞，让律动悬在人声与机械之间。那些偏斜的制作手法，常常承载着他松弛唱腔与不炫技演奏所掩饰的动荡。在 2020 年专辑《WILL THIS MAKE ME GOOD》的高潮曲《QADIR》中，被压低音高的“恶魔”叠声与无词合唱，把冰冷的恐惧推成灼热的绝望。

他的第四张录音室专辑《I Can See》大体缺少这类戏剧性，评论认为这也是其失分之处。这些歌都栖身于温和的中段地带：介于声响脏污与清晰之间，介于锐利钩子与模糊氛围之间。节奏始终克制适中；Hakim 的声音比以往更收敛，无论在演绎还是在混音位置上都是如此。细听仍能感到张力——《The Reason》副歌里令人不安的合成贝斯掠过，《Endlessly Waiting》中钢琴与合成器近距和声来回滴答，以及标题曲上或许被轻微 Chipmunk 处理的人声。但总体而言，那些粗糙质感被均质化、均匀铺开。磁带般的嘶声是专辑最先响起的声音，并贯穿始终，把所有歌曲裹进愉悦的劣化雾气里。宣传文案称这张专辑“药用”；若果真如此，评论暗示，这剂药多半是安眠药。

凭借对重复的执着、对极端的排斥，以及对声音物质感的迷恋——Hakim 与长期合作者 Andrew Sarlo 想让你感受到每个琴键的吱嘎与每道锯齿波的轰鸣——《I Can See》具备某种新灵魂极简主义的原料。Hakim 曾把 Steve Reich 与 Terry Riley 列为影响来源，可这些歌很少真正推进；它们大多只是继续前行，结束时几乎还在起点。即便如此，评论者在林中戴耳机散步、厨房煮茶放音箱、开车听立体声、合上电脑屏幕闭眼聆听之后，仍感到一种小小的奇迹：无论怎样用力贴近，音乐总退到注意力的中景，从而在每一次聆听中显得崭新。
